# Reviewer scorecards

Open Admin → an application → Assessment scorecard. Drafts are private and versioned. A stale browser cannot overwrite a newer draft. Public evidence text must be redacted before saving; the preview shows exactly the scored criteria and narrative that will be published. No credentials or automatic machine findings are promoted into public evidence.

Pilot v0.2 keeps the draft weights and publishes detailed rules at `/scoring-rules`. Four-check criteria accept 0/25/50/75/100; three-check criteria accept 0/33.33/66.67/100. Measured fractions and diversity are reviewer-entered results with documented calculations. The software bounds and weights numbers; it does not independently verify the underlying claim. Missing criteria leave an axis unassessed. A zero requires reviewed evidence of failure, not missing information.

Publishing requires an approved application, profile publication consent, a linked published profile, the exact saved version, reviewer confirmation, all eight criteria, public scope and change reason, 30 calendar days of observation, and observation/review dates within 30 days. Publication is transactional and repeat requests are idempotent. Published versions remain separate from draft edits; the last 50 publications and their reasons are retained for the administrator. Removing consent or approval hides the public scorecard. Publication does not change credentials or award higher tiers.

Pool profiles link to published scorecards. The scorecard shows its scope, reviewer, dates, evidence and totals. Observations older than 30 days are labeled historical and due for re-review. Formal higher-tier badges remain disabled. The existing participation badges and block-production ranks retain their distinct meanings.

Soveroot has an unsaved starter example when no draft exists: its dated gateway connection inspection, limitations and remaining ownership/template checks. No criterion receives points by default. The admin must save and review this example; the known inspection alone is insufficient for publishing a numerical score.
